## 1. Register-row drafts

**1xx — Hands inside the scene are solid: `ghost.alpha` is not applied there (no sort as one see-through object)**
- **Built:** with `Wiring(hands="scene")` the scene node draws the hands first (`Ghost.draw_solid`: the style's parts, depth test and depth write on, premultiplied over the cleared ground), then the see-through shape painter-sorted and depth-tested against them (A2's depth pass then writes the shape's visible surface). A hand in front of the shape hides it; behind it the shape's strokes and fills lie over it. `ghost.alpha` still sets the hands' *layer* (over / through / the glass); inside the scene the hands are at full opacity, each hand still premultiplied by its fade, so a hand entering or leaving is see-through for its 0.1 / 0.3 s fade. The guide and the tethers never go into the scene: they are the readout, laid on at the outputs.
- **Other options:** the hands sorted as one see-through object among the shape's faces (a sort key per hand, the hands drawn at ghost.alpha between the faces farther and nearer than them — needs the shape's face/edge draws split around the hand, a pass per hand; the hand's own overlaps show unless drawn to an offscreen first); order-independent transparency (deferred by decision 3).
- **To reverse:** local to `Ghost.draw_solid` and `graph.Scene.run` (a per-hand split of `draw_scene`'s draws).
- **Rough edge:** a fading hand writes depth at any fade, so for its fade-out (0.3 s) it cuts the shape behind it while it is already faint. `ghost.alpha` does nothing to in-scene hands **except at exactly 0**: `Ghost.poses` returns nothing when the alpha is 0 (as it always has for the layer), so dragging Ghost opacity to 0 removes the hands from the scene too, while 0.01 shows them fully solid — kept deliberately (0 = "no hands anywhere" is the one consistent reading; a separate in-scene opacity is A4/plan 28's). With the look-back on (party-wake, party-tracer) in-scene hands are in the history, so their echoes stack and a held hand saturates towards white.
- **Judge it:** V.12.

**1xx — The depth-aware composite hides a layer texel where the scene's visible surface is nearer by more than 0.05 shape radii**
- **Built:** `render.LAYER_FS` / `Renderer.lay(blend="depth")`: both depths linearised through the eye's clip planes; a layer texel is dropped (the picture under it shows exactly) when the scene wrote depth there (its visible surface, alpha > `DEPTH_ALPHA` 0.25, A2) and that surface is nearer than the layer's depth by more than `render.LAYER_DEPTH_BIAS` = 0.05 world units (≈ 8–10 mm of hand at the default calibration). Where the scene wrote no depth, or the layer's resolved depth is the far plane (a multisampled silhouette pixel whose picked depth sample missed the hand), the layer shows. Used by the through composite (`composite(scene+hands)` / `composite(hands)`), the history's composite and an output's hands-over when `Wiring.blend` is "depth". The scene's depth is resolved out of its MSAA target on the first ask of a frame (`SceneTarget.depth_texture`, made lazily: nothing is allocated or blitted unless a depth-aware node exists); the hands' layer now resolves its depth beside its colour every frame (`Ghost.depth`).
- **Other options:** compare raw window depths (bias not in world units: varies with distance); a soft fade over a depth range (wider halos at intersections, more to tune); hide on the far-plane silhouette too (measured: 29–48 rim pixels hidden even with the hands wholly in front).
- **To reverse:** one constant; the comparison is two lines of shader.
- **Rough edge:** silhouette pixels use one depth sample of four, so where a hand passes behind an edge the hidden boundary is aliased (1 px stair-step), and a hand behind the shape keeps a 1 px partially-covered rim. A presenter output (another GL context) lays a depth-aware layer plain over: the depth textures stay in the renderer's context. ("add" does reach a presenter: fix round 1 carries `Handoff.blend` and `Presenter` honours it.)
- **Judge it:** V.13.

**1xx — The OUTPUT switches are the default graph's wiring; a through pipeline "stands down" per frame**
- **Built:** `graph.default_graph(views, wiring)` wires each output from its switches: `image` = the SCENE chain (scene on), `fx(scene+hands)` (scene + hands + through: the hands' layer composited onto the history before the effects — "through"), `fx(hands)` (scene off, hands + through), or nothing (the ground); `hands` = the hands' layer laid over at the output ("over": hands on, through off); `readout` = the layer's readout while hands is on. `outputs.route`'s per-frame collapse — with no effect on, or no hand drawn, through is hands over — is wiring too: a through `Fx` node carries `fallback` (the SCENE chain, or the ground) and is `bent` only while an effect is on and its layer drew; otherwise its outputs take the fallback with the layer over (`Output.settle`). `Frame.routes` / `users(key)` are gone (users are per end node). `outputs.route` stays the pure table and the golden tool asserts the graph's settled route equals it for every default state (`stats["routes"]`). `outputs.py` still imports only `glsl`.
- **Other options:** through as a static wire that always runs the composite (no collapse — the "through with no effect" outputs would composite the hands into the picture before scaling instead of laying them at the output: pixels move for letterboxed, flipped and overlay outputs, and two outputs differing only by the through switch would run the chain twice); a new switch for "hands in the scene" per output (needs output.N params and a preset/session decision — A4's graph JSON instead).
- **To reverse:** local to `default_graph`, `Fx.bent`, `Output.settle`.
- **Rough edge:** the collapse is still a per-frame decision; a node editor (plan 28) will have to show a through wire that is sometimes not taken.
- **Judge it:** V.5 (A1's, unchanged), V.14.

**1xx — `Wiring`: where the hands are drawn, how their layer is laid on, what the history holds (renderer-wide, default today's)**
- **Built:** `graph.Wiring(hands="layer"|"scene", blend="over"|"add"|"depth", history="scene"|"scene+hands")`, held by `Renderer.wiring` (default `Wiring()` = today's frame); the graph is rebuilt when it changes. Nothing in the app sets it yet: A4 reads it (or a fuller graph) from a preset's `graph` section; tests and the golden tool set it directly. `hands="scene"`: an output showing the scene shows the hands in it (no layer over it, no SCENE+HANDS pipeline); an output with the scene off still gets the hands' layer over or through — the projector/glass split of decision 3 with one `Wiring`. `history="scene+hands"`: the capture takes `composite(history)`, the layer over the scene (materialised into a frame texture once a frame) — with the hands in the scene the scene already holds them.
- **Other options:** per-output wiring (the history and the scene are shared, so only blend could be per output — A4 can add it with node instances); a param (`render.*`) for each choice (would put the wiring in the registry and presets before A4 decides the graph JSON).
- **To reverse:** local; the dataclass is the seam A4 replaces or fills.
- **Rough edge:** with the hands in the scene, an output's **hands** switch no longer removes them from that output's scene (it only wires the readout and, scene off, the layer); there is no "scene without hands" for the history in that wiring.
- **Judge it:** V.12, V.15.

**1xx — The default calibration fits the padded box into a 16:9 picture of the eye (A2 fitted the shorter side's square)**
- **Supersedes** A2's calibration row's "fills the square on the shorter side" and V.11's wording of it: the docs WP (A5) should merge A2's calibration row and this one into a single row (A2: the `units` entry, the schema, the tilt; A3: the 16:9 fit) and reword V.11.
- **Built:** `ghost.calibration` from the box (`Units.mm` null) now fits every corner of the padded box into the eye's frame of a 16:9 reference picture (`ghost.CAL_ASPECT`, the party projector, as PAN_REF_PX and the 1080p cap), as tightly as the old ghost camera framed it there. Measured with the goldens' hands (pinned at 5 / 10 / 5 px for 960×600 / 1920×1080 / 1512×982): at 960×600 the hands' extent is 354–609 × 256–357 px against 350–613 × 254–358 through the old camera (≤ 4 px); at 1920×1080, ≤ 9 px; A2's shorter-side square had drawn them at 176 px wide instead of 263 (two thirds). `hands.json` is untouched (the default is "from the box"); an explicit `mm` still wins.
- **Other options:** keep the square (hands visibly smaller on every landscape output); fit to the actual output's aspect each frame (as the old camera did — then the hands' size in the world, relative to the shape, would change with the window, which the shared world forbids).
- **To reverse:** one constant (and the fit's two tangents).
- **Rough edge:** on a portrait or square output the box is wider than the frame: a hand at the box's left or right edge leaves the picture (the old camera re-fitted per output). The perspective is the eye's 47.6°, not 38°: the hands are slightly more foreshortened (fingers nearer the eye a touch bigger).
- **Judge it:** V.11 (A2's: hands where they were), V.16.

**1xx — The hands node is `ghost` (geometry) feeding the `hands` layer; its time is its own line only inside a scene**
- **Built:** the default graph gains a geometry node `ghost` (the `ghost.*` params' node; a second would be `ghost.2` by the naming rule) before the scene; the hands' layer keeps its name and SYSTEM line `hands`. Inside a scene the hands' draw is timed separately as `ghost` (the scene's timing is split around it); with the readout alone (hands only in the scene) a `readout` node and line. The SYSTEM page's default lines are unchanged (`scene cover hands history fx capture output.1`).
- **Other options:** name the geometry `hands` and the layer `layer(hands)` (renames today's SYSTEM line); always time `ghost` (a dead 0.00 line in the default graph).
- **To reverse:** names only.
- **Rough edge:** `Hands.TIMED` is set by the scene that holds it (a node's timing depends on its consumer); with one Hands node feeding both a scene and a layer (the in-scene + glass wiring) the `ghost` line is only its in-scene draws, its layer draw is in `hands`. A4 must fix this when nodes become data (timing owned by the node that draws, or per consumer edge).
- **Judge it:** V.4 (A1's), V.17.
