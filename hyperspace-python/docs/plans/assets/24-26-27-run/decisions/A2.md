## 1. Register-row drafts

**1xx — The eye: `eye.fov` and `eye.distance`, session, defaults that are the old 3D divide**
- **Built:** a fixed perspective camera on the world's +z axis looking at the origin, y up (`geometry.eye_matrix`). `eye.fov` = 47.61° (`geometry.EYE_FOV`) is the field of view across the **shorter** side of the picture (the longer side sees more, as project_h's `min(w, h)·0.30` did); `eye.distance` = 3.778 shape radii (`EYE_DISTANCE` = CAM3 / 0.9). Ranges 10–120° and 1.5–40 (log). Group `eye`, added to `params.SESSION_GROUPS`: a preset never moves it, the project's session saves it. Not modulatable (the camera is the rig; the shape is what moves). Clip planes 0.05 / 1000 (constants, `EYE_NEAR` / `EYE_FAR`: depth only, never seen). On the CAMERA page under an "eye" heading. The owner sees nothing change at the defaults.
- **Other options:** the eye's fov on the vertical (would not reproduce project_h on a portrait output); an `eye.*` position/target/up set (more knobs than a fixed camera needs; A3/A4 can add them); modulatable eye params (a lane would swing the whole room).
- **To reverse:** local (two params, `eye_matrix`'s arguments).
- **Rough edge:** moving the eye off its defaults moves the shape's whole picture (no golden covers it). `camera.zoom` reaches 40× in the world: past about 3.8× the eye is inside the shape (strokes behind the eye are cut, `EYE_W`).
- **Judge it:** V.6.

**1xx — `camera.pan` in world units (±12 shape radii), marked by a `units` key, converted through the 1080p screen**
- **Built:** `camera.pan.x/y` are the shape's world position in shape radii (`geometry.model_matrix`), y positive **down** the screen as it always was (so stored and routed pans keep their direction; world y is up, so the matrix moves by −pan.y). Range ±`params.PAN_RANGE` = ±12, format `+0.00`. Migration in `preset.load_doc` step 0 beside `echo_seconds` (`preset.pan_world`): a document **without** `"units": {"camera.pan": "world"}` stored logical px, divided by `geometry.PAN_REF_PX` = 0.30 × 1080 = 324 px a unit (the default eye's px per unit at the origin on the 1080p render cap / the party projector). Silent (no note: factory files are read this way on every load; decision 23 keeps them byte-identical). `Presets.document` writes the key on every save; the schema stays `hyperspace.preset/2` (an older build ignores the key and would read a world pan as px — 2.5 px, a non-event). Only `values` convert: route / desk depths and macro base-subscriber from/to are fractions of the range (`Sub.at` lands a norm), and ±12 was chosen so the range reaches ±3888 px at 1080p where ±4000 px did. hands-orbit's push 0.1 moves the shape ±1.2 radii (≈ ±390 px at 1080p; ±400 px before). The mouse drag pans by cursor px / px-per-unit and the wheel zooms about the point under the cursor on the plane through the origin (`app._px_per_unit`).
- **Other options:** a schema bump to preset/3 (an older build would then refuse every new file); the reference screen = the window's size at load (a preset would land differently per screen, the problem px had); the golden size 960×600 (party-mandala's golden would not have moved, the projector would); pan.y positive up (every routed and stored pan.y would flip direction); converting route depths (would change factory routes' meaning, and the range makes it unnecessary).
- **To reverse:** `pan_world` and the range; saved world-unit files would need the opposite conversion (the key says which they are).
- **Rough edge:** on a screen other than 1080p a converted pan lands proportionally: **on the laptop window (or any window shorter than 1080 logical px) the shape sits proportionally nearer the centre than it did before** — a 190 px pan was 190 px on every screen, it is now 0.59 radii, which is 190 px at 1080p and 105 px at 960×600 (party-mandala's golden moved for this). The same holds for an old camera module preset (the CAMERA strip), converted the same way since review fix 1 (`Library._apply_doc` / `defaults` through `params.pan_world`; a camera module saved now carries the marker). A pan is exact at depth 0; off-centre the shape is now seen a little from the side (perspective), so its silhouette is not a 2D shift.
- **Judge it:** V.8, V.9.

**1xx — camera.zoom is the shape's world scale; strokes and dots keep their pixel weight; copies live in the world**
- **Built:** `model_matrix` scales the shape about the world origin (no more 2D zoom of the picture). The line width and the point radius are no longer × zoom. Instances are copies in the world: a cell is `render.CELL` = 0.72 / 0.30 = 2.4 radii (the old `0.72·min(w, h)` px through the default eye), offsets before the zoom, so the field is scaled and moved as one and at zoom 1 is spaced exactly as before at depth 0. The perspective of a scaled shape is its own (a small zoom is flatter, a big one deeper), and off-centre copies are seen slightly from the side.
- **Other options:** copies as screen-space tiles after the projection (today's picture exactly, but the copies would not be in the world the hands join); the cell fixed in the world with the zoom only on each copy (zooming out would spread a field apart).
- **To reverse:** the cell offsets inside `u_model` vs outside; the width/radius `× zoom` back in `draw_scene`.
- **Rough edge:** at the zoomed-out factory looks (ember-orbit 0.5, hex-engine 0.4, party-field 0.42) the red dots are now 2–2.5× the size they were and the edges heavier — the decision's "line width no longer scales with zoom" applies to dots too.
- **Judge it:** V.7.

**1xx — The shape's depth: the see-through shape is never depth-tested against itself; its visible surface (alpha > 0.25) is written after its colour**
- **Built:** `SceneTarget` has a depth attachment (single-sampled: a depth texture, `SceneTarget.depth`; multisampled: a depth renderbuffer on the MSAA framebuffer). `draw_scene` draws faces, edges, points painter-sorted as before, with the depth test on (against whatever solid geometry is already there — A3's hands) and depth writes off; then `Renderer._depth_pass` draws them again with the blend `(ZERO, ONE)` (colour untouched) writing depth only for fragments whose alpha is over `render.DEPTH_ALPHA` = **0.25**. A draw that cannot reach it is skipped (fills at brightness ≤ ~1.4 never do). With no depth attachment (`render_offscreen`, a bare target) nothing changes. No golden moved for "depth-tested overlap": the colour pass is byte-identical with or without a depth attachment (pinned).
- **Other options:** depth writes in the colour pass (edges crossing in the midpoint sort's wrong order would cut each other — the painter sort would be replaced by the depth test, which the decision forbids); threshold 0.1 (bright fills would hide a hand behind a barely visible face) or 0.5 (only the nearest edges would count).
- **To reverse:** one constant; the pass is one method.
- **Rough edge:** the depth pass is extra GPU work for a depth no node reads until A3, on almost every frame: the edges and the points re-draw whenever their `alpha.max()` is over 0.25 (nearly always — the nearest edge sits at B = bright × density), and the faces re-run the pattern shader whenever `fa.max() · 2.8` is over 0.25 (not only at bright 2: any preset whose nearest fill can reach it). **Measured** (review fix 1; the `scene` node's GPU median from the per-node timer, 240 frames, 1920×1080, 4× MSAA, hands absent, two runs each): party-trip 1.03 / 0.45 ms with the pass, 0.90 / 0.48 ms without; party-mandala 3.25 / 2.94 ms with, 3.31 / 3.31 ms without — the difference is inside run-to-run noise (±0.1–0.4 ms) on the M3 Max. Bright fills (alpha up to ~0.36 at bright 2) do write depth. The skip bound `fa.max() · 2.8` rests on `FACE_PX` clamping the pattern's value to [0, 1] (`v = clamp(v, 0.0, 1.0)`, already in the shader: no golden can move for it) — a `patterns.glsl` cannot exceed it; a future face shader that lets shade pass 2.8 must raise the bound.
- **Judge it:** V.10 (A3's hands between edges).

**1xx — Point quads are a whole number of pixels across**
- **Built:** `draw_scene` rounds each point's diameter to whole framebuffer pixels (`floor(2r + 0.5) / 2`). Measured: Apple's driver rounded `gl_PointSize`; with the rounding the quads draw the old sprites' frames byte for byte (legacy6, lfo-breathe, hands-*), without it every dot grew by up to a pixel.
- **Other options:** the true fractional size (every points-on golden moves; dots a little bigger).
- **To reverse:** one line.
- **Rough edge:** dots still change size in one-pixel steps with depth, as they did.
- **Judge it:** V.6.

**1xx — The Leap calibration: `units` in hands.json /3 — mm per shape unit, the Leap origin, the tilt; "from the box" by default**
- **Built:** `hands.core.Units(mm, origin, tilt)` in `hands.json` beside the ranges and the box: `"units": {"mm": null, "origin": null, "tilt": 32.0}`. A Leap point p lands at `world = R_x(tilt) · (stage(p) − c) / mm` (`ghost.calibration`), stage = the tracking mode's basis with `ghost.mirror`. `mm` null → the scale at which the padded palm box (`ghost.stage_box`) fills the **square on the shorter side** of the eye's frame (independent of an output's shape); `origin` null → the padded box's centre; `tilt` 32° = `ghost.ELEVATION`, so the eye on the world axis sees the hands from above as the ghost camera does. `CAL_SCHEMA` = `hyperspace.hands/3`: a /2 (or /1) file is read as it is and kept as `hands.json.bak-v2` (`-v1`) by the first save (the existing `_keep`). A bad entry falls back to "from the box" with one stderr line. Not editable in the UI yet.
- **Other options:** the calibration as a fit to each output's aspect (as `ghost.camera` is: a room setting would then depend on the output); a full 4×4 in the file (opaque to edit); an anisotropic map that reproduces `ghost.camera` exactly (see §4: hands depth would be squashed in the world).
- **To reverse:** local to `Units`, `calibration`, `save_calibration`; the /3 files keep their axes and box.
- **Rough edge:** nothing reads `units` for drawing in A2. When A3 moves the hands into the eye they will be seen at 47.6° (shorter side) instead of the ghost camera's 38° (vertical) and framed on the shorter side's square instead of the output's frame: the hands' picture changes slightly (§4).
- **Judge it:** V.11.

**1xx — A stroke end behind the eye is slid to just in front of it**
- **Built:** in `LINE_VS` an edge end with clip w below `EYE_W` = 1.2 × EYE_NEAR is moved along the edge to w = EYE_W; an edge wholly behind is dropped; a point behind the eye is dropped. Faces are clipped by the GPU.
- **Other options:** let the GPU clip the expanded quad (an end behind the eye projects to the wrong side and the quad sweeps the screen).
- **To reverse:** local to the shader.
- **Rough edge:** only reachable past zoom ≈ 3.8 (the eye inside the shape).
- **Judge it:** V.7.

## 4. Not done / where the prompt and the code differ

- **`ghost.camera` is expressible from `eye.*`, but not equal to it.** A3's wiring: in `Ghost.poses`, `vp, eye = camera(box, m, aspect)` becomes `vp, eye = shared_camera(box, m, aspect, getattr(self.source, "units", None), fov=view.eye_fov, distance=view.eye_distance)` — same arguments plus the units and the eye, same return shape (view-proj taking stage points, eye in stage space), the poses and the guide unchanged. The hands' picture then changes: the eye's field of view is fixed at 47.6° on the shorter side by the parity bar (F = 2·SCALE·distance is forced by project_h), the ghost camera's is 38° vertical and fitted to the output's frame. A similarity calibration (mm into shape units, which is what the decision asks for) cannot turn one perspective into the other; the exact match needs an anisotropic map (view-space x, y scaled by tan(23.8°)/tan(19°) ≈ 1.28 against z) plus box-dependent clip planes, which would squash the hands' depth in the world the shape shares. So **A3's "default graphs must still match A2's recordings" will not hold for the frames with hands** unless A3 keeps the hands' layer on `ghost.camera`; the orchestrator should give A3 a reason to re-record the hands frames (perspective 38° → 47.6°, framing on the shorter side's square). `tests/test_camera.py::test_the_shared_camera_frames_the_padded_box_like_the_ghost_camera` pins what does match: the box's centre at the picture's centre, the eye 32° above and behind the hands, the padded box in frame touching the shorter side's square, up up and right right, `ghost.mirror` mirroring.
- **No "depth-tested overlap" moves.** The prompt expected goldens to move where geometry crosses; the decision also says the painter sort is not replaced by the depth test. I read the second as binding: the see-through shape is never depth-tested against itself, so nothing moved for that reason (the colour is byte-identical with or without a depth attachment, pinned).
- **No preset schema bump** (a key marks the unit, see the row).
- **`ENGINEERING.md` §2 not edited** (the brief forbids; the docs WP lifts §3 above).
- **The contact sheet** is my own compact one (`docs/plans/assets/26-render-graph/a2-moved.png`, 1.6 MB: each moved preset's solo frame with hands, before | after | diff ×4); the tool's own sheet of all 184 moved frames was 13 MB and is not committed (regenerate it with `git checkout 14834c2 -- tests/golden/frames && uv run python tools/golden_frames.py`).
- **The windowed smoke run's picture** could not be captured (see §3).
