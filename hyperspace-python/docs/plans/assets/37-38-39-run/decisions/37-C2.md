# 37-C2 decisions: hover gives details; the "pictures · tint cables" switch

Branch `plan-37-c2-hover` off `plan-37-pictures` @ 761aef3 (commits 68335cf, e1a05e4), PR #258.

## 1. Register-row drafts (4xx; the docs task renumbers them within 417–429)

**4xg: What hovering a picture node's thumbnail says.**
**Answer** (controller, 2026-10-27): `graphview.picture_hover`, called from `ui_graph.thumb_tip`, gives these lines in order: the node's title; the lines `graphview.summary` gives a collapsed node (param counts, "N picture ports", the node's `said`, a switch's "picks inK", "reaches nothing"); `W×H picture`, the size the thumbnail was last copied from (`VNode.picture`; output 1 takes it from the `Preview` texture's size); `gpu N.NN ms`, the SYSTEM page's rolling median (`Renderer.stats["gpu_ms"]`, keyed by the render node's name, `thumb_name`); and the node's status when it has one. A line with nothing behind it is left out. The tooltip shows only while the pointer is on the picture (`imgui.is_item_hovered` on the thumbnail's box). On the title, a picture node hovers as before (compile error, faint tip, status). Non-picture nodes are unchanged.
**Built**: point at a picture and you get its N7 lines (C1 took them off the collapsed body), its size, and what it costs on the GPU.
**Other options**: the hover on the whole node rather than just its picture; the summary drawn under the thumbnail (which loses the room C1 gave the picture).
**To reverse**: one branch in `_hover`, or the lines in `picture_hover`.
**Rough edge**: the title repeats on line 1, as every other node tooltip does. A geometry shown as its scene's picture reports its own GPU line only when it is timed under its own name (a solid geometry); otherwise no time line. An effect that did not run a pass this frame has no time line. When a picture node is faint, hovering its picture says "reaches nothing" but not the FAINT_TIP advice, which hovering its title still gives.
**Judge it**: AH.4.

**4xh: The tint: red leans by the picture's hue, at most 20°, continuously; read from the thumbnail asynchronously, once a second.** (revised in review round 1)
**Answer** (controller, 2026-10-27): `ui_graph.tint(hue, strength)` moves red's hue (`view.RED`, hue 0) by `TINT_SPAN` (20°) × sin(2π·(hue − red)) × min(1, strength / `TINT_FULL` (0.25)). Saturation and value stay red's. The lean is continuous all the way round the circle. The yellow-green half leans toward orange, most at chartreuse (90°): yellow gives about +17° and orange about +10°. The blue-violet half leans toward rose, most at violet (270°). Red, cyan and grey pictures give no lean, so a cyan-ish moving picture never flips from one end to the other. The tint stays more than 10° from the trigger amber and 60° from the mask purple (tested). The hue comes from `outputs.dominant_hue`, a chroma-weighted circular mean: each texel is the vector (r − (g+b)/2, √3/2·(g−b)) and the hue is the angle of their sum. The read is asynchronous and done in `PreviewTextures.begin_frame` (`_read_hue`). First it collects the hue started the frame before: `buf.read()` of a 1 KB pixel buffer the GPU filled a frame ago. Then, while the page asks (`want_hues` each frame the switch is lit; `stop_hues` the frame it goes off), it starts at most one hue: the thumbnail whose hue is oldest and at least `HUE_EVERY_S` (1 s) old gets one `THUMB_FS` pass into a 16×16 target and `fbo.read_into(buffer)`, which is a PBO copy with no wait. No texture is ever `read()` (tested with a counter).
**Cost**: while lit, one 16×16 pass and a 1 KB asynchronous copy per frame at most, plus the collect a frame later. Weighing 256 texels in pure Python takes well under a millisecond. Each node is refreshed once a second. With the switch off, nothing is read; a hue already in flight is still collected.
**Built**: with the switch lit, a graph of pictures shows its reds leaning orange or rose by what flows.
**Other options**: the first cut was a linear signed arc onto ±20° (it flipped at cyan and followed distance from red rather than warmth) read with a blocking `tex.read()`, which reviewers found was effectively a `ctx.finish()` each frame it read. Other options considered: an RGB mix with the picture's colour (a blue picture would give a purple cable, the mask's colour); a full hue; mipmaps on the thumbnail.
**To reverse**: two constants, or `tint` itself.
**Rough edge**: a ±20° lean is subtle by design. A red picture and a cyan one both leave the cable plain red. The hue lags the picture by one frame plus up to a second. If the GPU is more than a frame behind, the collect `buf.read()` waits for that one copy.
**Judge it**: AH.1 (the tint line), AH.2.

**4xi: The switch is off by default, and its state lives on the page.**
**Answer** (controller, 2026-10-27): `st["tint"]`, kept in the GRAPH page's state on the panel (`_state`, `setdefault(False)`) the same way `backdrop` and `patched_only` are. It is saved with neither the preset nor the show, because the show keeps no GRAPH page state (a test saves both, with the switch on and off, and compares). Hue reads stop the frame the switch goes off (`stop_hues`). The chip is `pictures · tint cables`, last on the head (`_tint_chip`, one call in `_head` after plan 36's chips and the details chip) so that plan 38's hold chips land before it in a one-line merge. It survives a hot reload of `ui_graph` but not a restart.
**Built**: plain red until you light it, per run.
**Other options**: a preset value (the owner judges by eye, so it is a view setting rather than part of the show); a config.toml key.
**To reverse**: a `setdefault`.
**Rough edge**: an owner who likes it has to light it again after every launch.
**Judge it**: AH.1 (the tint line).

## 3. As-built notes for C2

**As built.**
- `graphview.picture_hover(n, gpu_ms)`, the hover text.
- `ui_graph`: `thumb_tip`, `_tint_chip`, `tint`, `cable_tints`, `TINT_LABEL`/`TINT_TIP`/`TINT_SPAN`/`TINT_FULL`. `_state` gains `tint`, `thumb_hover` and `tints`. `_canvas` keeps `st["gpu_ms"]`, resets `thumb_hover` before drawing the nodes, calls `want_hues` while the switch is lit, and computes `st["tints"]` after `_wiring` (so the through wires are known). `_thumb` marks `thumb_hover`. `_hover` has the new branch. `_cable` takes its colour from `tints` when the cable has one. `_want_thumbs` gives output 1's node its `Preview` size.
- `outputs`: `HUE_EVERY_S`, `HUE_GRID`, `dominant_hue`, `PreviewTextures.want_hues`/`stop_hues`/`hue`/`_read_hue` (called from `begin_frame`)/`_hue_collect`/`_hue_start`, and `_pass`, split out of `_copy` so both use C1's downsample pass. A 16×16 target and a 1 KB buffer are made the first time and released in `close`. `drop` forgets a hue.
- Tests: `tests/test_graph_hover.py` (11).

**Differs from the spec / brief.**
- The hover's first line is the title, as every node tooltip has it; the title also stays on the node.
- The hue is read asynchronously: a 16×16 pass into a pixel buffer, collected a frame later, at most one node a frame. `render.py` is untouched.
- The tint is a ±20° lean, 20° × sin(hue − red), rather than a colour "bled" in, so red stays red. It is continuous, and red and cyan pictures give no lean.

**Not verified by a person.** The tooltip inside the node editor at the machine (it is driven headless through a stand-in hover); how the tint reads by eye; the readback's cost on the show Mac.

**Known rough edges.** See rows 4xg–4xi. Bundles are value and trigger only, so they are never tinted. A through wire is tinted by its source node's hue.
